When you search for "organic green tea" on Google, the top results don't get there by chance. Behind them there's often the work of an SEO specialist: someone who understands **what people type** and prepares the website's pages to answer better than anyone else.

## What is SEO?

SEO stands for *Search Engine Optimisation*. It shouldn't be confused with SEA, paid advertising on search engines.

|  | SEO | SEA (ads) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cost** | You don't pay for clicks | You pay for every click |
| **Timing** | A few months | Straight away |
| **Where it shows** | Normal results | "Sponsored" results |
| **When you stop** | Visits keep coming for a long time | Visits stop |

## What do they do every day?

1. Checks in Search Console how visits from Google went.
2. Looks for new keywords for product pages.
3. Rewrites the title and description of a page that gets few clicks.
4. Checks that new pages have been indexed, that is read by Google.
5. Talks with the content writers to plan the next articles.

## The 3 parts of the job

Almost everything an SEO specialist does falls into one of these three parts.

- **Content**: chooses keywords and writes or improves copy, titles and descriptions.
- **Technical**: checks that the website is fast, error-free and that Google can read it.
- **Authority**: makes sure other trusted websites talk about the site and link to it.

## Words to know

- **Keyword**: the word or phrase a person types into Google, for example "organic green tea".
- **SERP**: the Google results page (*Search Engine Results Page*).
- **Title tag**: the blue title of the result. Google measures it in pixels: beyond about 580 pixels it gets cut off with "...".
- **Meta description**: the grey text under the title. It doesn't improve your ranking, but it convinces people to click.
- **Search intent**: what the person searching wants to do: learn, compare or buy.

## Which tools do they use?

To start, three free Google tools are enough.

- **Google Search Console**: shows which searches the website appears for, how many clicks it gets and whether there are errors.
- **Google Trends**: shows whether a search is growing and in which months people search for it most.
- **Google Keyword Planner**: suggests keywords and rough search volumes. You need a Google Ads account, but you don't have to spend anything.

## A step-by-step example

Cinque Foglie is the practice tea shop of Marketing Mix. The Sencha tea page gets very few visits. Here's what the SEO specialist does:

1. **Understands who searches for what.** "Green tea" is searched by many people, but to learn. "Sencha green tea" is searched by people who want to compare or buy: it's the right keyword for a product page.
2. **Rewrites the title.** Before it was just "Sencha". Now it's "Organic Loose Leaf Sencha Green Tea | Cinque Foglie": keyword first, brand last, under 580 pixels.
3. **Checks the rules.** No health claims like "burns fat": for food, in the European Union they are only allowed if authorised (Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006).
4. **Measures the result.** After a few weeks, checks in Search Console whether clicks and position have improved.

**Before:** "Sencha" · "Green tea. Buy now."
**After:** "Organic Loose Leaf Sencha Green Tea | Cinque Foglie" · "Organic Japanese Sencha with whole leaves and a fresh, grassy taste. Great iced too. 24-hour delivery."

## How do you become an SEO specialist?

You don't need a specific degree. You need clear basics, plenty of practice and a way to show what you can do.

1. **Learn the basics**: articles like this one, Google's free guides and a glossary.
2. **Practise**: on a real website or on a practice one, like Cinque Foglie.
3. **Show your results**: explain in a portfolio what you changed and what happened next.

## Is it right for you?

Probably yes, if you recognise yourself in at least two of these sentences:

- You enjoy writing and finding the right word.
- You're curious about what people search for.
- You like looking at numbers and understanding why they change.
- You're patient: results take a few months.

A brand's name and logo are worth years of work. If a competitor used an almost identical name, customers could get confused. A **registered trademark** prevents that.

## What registration gives you

The exclusive right to use that sign (name, logo, sometimes a colour or sound) for the goods and services listed, and to oppose anyone using an identical or confusingly similar one.

## Where to register

| | Italian trademark | EU trade mark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Office** | UIBM (Italian Patent and Trademark Office) | EUIPO (EU Intellectual Property Office) |
| **Where it protects** | Italy | All EU countries |
| **Duration** | 10 years, renewable | 10 years, renewable |

For other countries there's the international system (WIPO), which starts from a national or EU registration.

## Before registering

1. **Search the databases** (UIBM, EUIPO, TMview) for identical or similar marks in the same sector.
2. **Choose the right classes**: for a tea shop, for example, the class for tea and the one for retail.
3. **Check distinctiveness**: a name that only describes the product ("Good Tea") is hard to register.

## Afterwards

- Actually use the trademark: one left unused for years can be revoked.
- Keep watch: if someone files a similar mark, you can oppose it within the deadlines.
- Renew before it expires.

You can file on your own, but for important decisions it's worth asking an intellectual property adviser.

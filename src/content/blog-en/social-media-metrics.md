Social media analytics are full of numbers. You don't need to read them all: you need to know **which number answers the question you're asking**.

## The main metrics

| Metric | What it measures |
| --- | --- |
| **Reach** | How many different people saw the content |
| **Impressions** | How many times it was seen in total (including repeat views) |
| **Likes and comments** | How much people liked it and talked about it |
| **Saves** | How useful it is: "I want to find this again" |
| **Shares** | How worth showing to others it is |
| **Non-followers** | What share of the audience didn't follow the account |
| **Link clicks** | How many people went to the website |

**Engagement rate** combines interactions: see [engagement rate](/en/blog/engagement-rate/).

## One metric per goal

| Goal | What to watch |
| --- | --- |
| Get known | Reach and share of non-followers |
| Be useful | Saves and shares |
| Build relationships | Comments and replies |
| Sell | Link clicks and sales (with UTM links) |

## Reading the numbers well

- **Compare with yourself.** Another account's numbers depend on its audience, sector and size.
- **Look for what the best and worst posts have in common**: format, topic, time.
- **Watch out for different causes.** In the fictional Cinque Foglie shop, posts had dropped because of the wrong time (3 am), but a 7 pm product photo did badly for another reason: it was an ad with no reason to stop scrolling.

## Measuring sales

Social platforms count clicks, the website counts sales. To connect them, use **UTM links**: they add information about where the visit comes from.

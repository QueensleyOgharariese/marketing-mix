When you think of a brand you know, a colour, a shape and a way of speaking come to mind. All of that together is the **brand identity**: the way a brand presents itself.

## Identity and image

| | Brand identity | Brand image |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Who decides it** | The company | People |
| **Where it lives** | In the brand guidelines | In customers' minds |
| **How you learn it** | By reading the guidelines | Through surveys, reviews, comments |

The brand manager's job is to bring them closer: to make what people think resemble what the brand wants to be.

## Elements of the identity

### Verbal

- **Name**: easy to say, spell and remember.
- **Tagline**: the short line next to the name.
- **Tone of voice**: how the brand speaks. See [tone of voice](/en/blog/tone-of-voice/).

### Visual

- **Logo**: always the same, in the planned versions (on light and dark backgrounds). Never stretched or recoloured.
- **Colours**: one main colour and a few supporting ones, with exact codes (for example #1F5C38).
- **Typefaces**: one or two fonts, always the same.
- **Imagery**: what kind of photos and illustrations to use.

### Underlying

- **Mission**: why the brand exists.
- **Values**: what it believes in, explained through concrete behaviour.

## Readability

A beautiful but unreadable identity doesn't work. Text and background need enough **contrast**: the web accessibility guidelines (WCAG) ask for a ratio of at least 4.5 to 1 for normal text. That applies to website, social media and packaging.

## An example

The fictional tea shop Cinque Foglie has three values: *clarity*, *respect for growers*, *curiosity*. The logo is five leaves in a circle, dark green on a light background. The main colour is a leaf green, with a cream and an almost-black for text. It's all in the [brand guidelines](/en/blog/brand-guidelines/), so anyone creating new material follows the same rules.

Think about the last thing you bought online. How did you find out about it? Why did you choose that one? Will you buy it again? Behind each of these answers there's someone working in marketing.

## What is marketing?

Marketing is the set of activities a company uses to **understand what people want**, get known, convince people to buy and make sure customers come back. It's not just advertising: it's also choosing the right product, the price, the channels and the way to talk to people.

## The 4 stages of the customer journey

Before buying, and coming back, a person goes through several stages. To keep it simple, we use four.

1. **Get known**: the person finds out that the brand exists.
2. **Get found**: when they search for something, they find us.
3. **Convince and sell**: they choose our product, online or in a shop.
4. **Bring back and measure**: they buy again, and we understand what worked.

## The 12 roles, stage by stage

| Stage | Roles | An example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Get known | Brand manager, Social media manager, Influencer marketing, PR and communication | Deciding the brand's tone, preparing posts, choosing a creator, writing a press release |
| Get found | SEO specialist, SEA / PPC specialist | Getting a page to appear on Google, for free or with ads |
| Convince and sell | Product marketing, Trade marketing, Partnerships | Launching a product, placing it on the shelf, working with another company |
| Bring back and measure | CRM manager, Market research, Marketing analytics / martech | Writing to customers, running a survey, reading the website's data |

## An example: one tea, twelve jobs

Cinque Foglie is the practice tea shop of Marketing Mix. To sell a new herbal tea, the brand manager decides how to talk about it and product marketing chooses its price and launch. The SEO specialist gets it found on Google, while the social media manager and the creator show it on Instagram. Trade marketing takes it to the supermarket. Then the CRM manager writes to customers, market research asks what they think and analytics reads the numbers to understand what worked.

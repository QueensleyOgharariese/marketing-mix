Two people type "green tea" into Google. The first wants to know how much caffeine it has, the second wants to buy a pack. Same words, different needs. That need is called **search intent**.

## The four types of intent

| Intent | The person wants to… | Example | Right page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Informational** | understand or learn | "how to make matcha" | Article or guide |
| **Navigational** | reach a specific website | "cinque foglie tea" | Home or a page of the site |
| **Commercial** | compare before choosing | "best organic green tea" | Buying guide, category |
| **Transactional** | buy or do something | "buy matcha kit" | Product page |

## How to recognise it

The most reliable method is the simplest: **search the keyword and look at Google's first page**.

- Lots of articles and videos? Informational intent.
- Shops, prices and product pages? Transactional intent.
- A mix? Mixed intent: better to choose a more specific keyword.

The words help too: "how", "what is", "why" suggest informational intent; "buy", "price", "deal" suggest transactional intent.

## Why it matters

A page that doesn't match the intent won't rank, even if it's well written. If someone searching "how to make matcha" lands on a product page, they go back: Google notices.

In the fictional Cinque Foglie shop, the Sencha product page and the Sencha guide were competing for the same search. The fix: the product page targets "organic Sencha tea" (buying), the guide targets "how to make Sencha" (learning), and the guide links to the product page.

Every sector has many competitors selling similar things. **Positioning** answers the question: *why should anyone choose us?*

## The formula

> For **[audience]**, **[brand]** is **[category]** that **[difference]**, because **[proof]**.

- **Audience**: a specific group, not "everyone".
- **Category**: which "shelf" the brand sits on.
- **Difference**: what it offers that competitors don't, and that matters to that audience.
- **Proof** (*reason to believe*): a verifiable fact that makes the difference credible.

## How to find it, in 3 steps

### 1. Choose the audience

Start from customer data. At the fictional tea shop Cinque Foglie, a survey shows almost half the customers (46%) drink tea every day and want to understand what they're buying.

### 2. Look at competitors

Put them in a table, with what matters to the audience:

| | Price | Explanations | Organic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Large tea chain | low | few | partly |
| Fine tea boutique | high | for experts | yes |
| Marketplace | low-medium | none | mixed |

The gap: **organic tea explained simply, at a mid-range price**.

### 3. Find the proof

A fact anyone can check: "every tea has a guide", "everything is certified organic".

## The result

> For people who drink tea every day and want to understand what they buy, Cinque Foglie is the online shop for organic teas and herbal teas that explains every tea simply, because every product has its own guide and everything is certified organic.

## How to test it

1. **Competitor test**: could the large chain sign it? No.
2. **Proof test**: can it be verified? Yes.
3. **Choice test**: does it say who we're not talking to? Yes, experts in rare teas.

Once written, the positioning guides everything else: [tone of voice](/en/blog/tone-of-voice/), copy, products, campaigns.

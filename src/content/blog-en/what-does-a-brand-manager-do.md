Why do you pick one coffee over another almost identical one? Often because of the idea you have of that brand: reliable, friendly, close to you. That idea is the **brand**, and the person who looks after it is the **brand manager**.

## What do they do every day?

1. Check that new materials (website, social, packaging) follow the brand guidelines.
2. Brief designers, copywriters and agencies.
3. Look at the data: surveys, NPS, branded searches, reviews.
4. Prepare presentations for management.
5. Step in when something could damage the brand's image.

## The parts of the job

- **Positioning**: who the brand is for and what makes it different. See [brand positioning](/en/blog/brand-positioning/).
- **Identity**: name, logo, colours, values, [tone of voice](/en/blog/tone-of-voice/), all collected in the [brand guidelines](/en/blog/brand-guidelines/).
- **Measurement**: awareness and trust. See [how to measure a brand](/en/blog/how-to-measure-a-brand/).
- **Protection**: the [registered trademark](/en/blog/how-to-protect-a-trademark/), accurate [green claims](/en/blog/green-claims/), [crisis communication](/en/blog/crisis-communication/).

## Words to know

- **Brand identity**: how the brand wants to be seen (decided by the company).
- **Brand image**: how people actually see it.
- **Positioning**: the space the brand occupies in people's minds compared with competitors.
- **Awareness**: how many people know the brand.
- **NPS**: how likely customers are to recommend it.

## An example

Cinque Foglie, Marketing Mix's fictional tea shop, has happy customers but a fuzzy idea of itself: everyone in the company describes it differently. The brand manager starts from the data (almost half the customers drink tea every day and want to understand what they buy; competitors either explain little or only talk to experts) and writes the positioning: *organic tea, explained simply*. From there she fixes the tone of the texts, the website's promise and the line on the packaging.

## Is it for you?

Probably, if at least two of these sound like you:

- You notice straight away when a text or design "doesn't fit" a brand.
- You like understanding why people choose a brand.
- You can explain an idea simply to different people.
- You're patient: a brand is built over years, not weeks.

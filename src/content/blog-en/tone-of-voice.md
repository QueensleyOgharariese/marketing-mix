Read a website's home page, then one of its emails, then a customer service reply: sometimes they sound like three different companies. **Tone of voice** prevents that: it defines how the brand speaks, everywhere.

## Why it matters

- **Recognition**: a brand that always speaks the same way is easier to remember.
- **Trust**: readers feel they're always talking to the same "person".
- **Speed**: writers already know what to do, without starting from scratch.

## How to define it, in 4 steps

### 1. Who we are

Three adjectives, each with a sentence explaining it. For the fictional tea shop Cinque Foglie:

- *Friendly but knowledgeable*: we know our stuff, without lecturing.
- *Simple*: short sentences, everyday words.
- *Calm*: no shouting, no fake urgency.

### 2. Who we're not

Just as important as step 1, because it removes doubt: *not cold or bureaucratic, not snobbish, not pushy*.

### 3. The rules

Few, concrete, checkable:

- Always informal (in Italian, "tu", never "Lei").
- No capitals for shouting and no "!!!".
- Emoji only on social, one per text at most.
- No health claims: tea and herbal teas are food.

### 4. Before and after examples

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| Dear customer, we inform you that the product is available. | Good news: your favourite tea is back. |
| SUPER DEAL!!! BUY NOW!!! | This week Sencha is 15% off. |

## How to check a text

A quick checklist:

1. Is it clear on first read?
2. Does it follow the rules (formality, capitals, emoji)?
3. Does it sound like the "after" examples?
4. With the logo covered, can you recognise the brand?

## Same tone, different intensity

On social media you can be a little lighter; when replying to a complaint, a little more serious. The personality stays the same. Tone, values, logo and colours are collected in the [brand guidelines](/en/blog/brand-guidelines/).

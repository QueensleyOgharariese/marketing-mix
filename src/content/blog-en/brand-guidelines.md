In a company many people write and design: marketing, customer service, agencies, freelance designers. Without shared guidance, everyone does their own thing and the brand frays. **Brand guidelines** (or a *brand book*) are that guidance.

## What they contain

1. **Who we are**: mission and values, with concrete behaviour.
2. **Positioning**: who we're for and what makes us different. See [brand positioning](/en/blog/brand-positioning/).
3. **Tagline**: the short line next to the name.
4. **Tone of voice**: who we are, who we're not, rules, before and after examples. See [tone of voice](/en/blog/tone-of-voice/).
5. **Logo**: versions, clear space, what not to do.
6. **Colours**: exact codes and readable combinations.
7. **Typefaces**: which ones, for headings and body text.
8. **Imagery**: what photos and illustrations to use.
9. **Examples**: posts, emails, packaging done right.

## Making them useful

- **Short**: the important rules in a few pages.
- **Concrete**: "always informal" is a rule; "we're warm" isn't.
- **With examples**: a before-and-after is worth more than a paragraph.
- **Easy to find**: a shared page, not a file lost in a folder.

## An example

The fictional tea shop Cinque Foglie's guidelines fit on one page: mission ("helping people who aren't experts discover good tea"), three values, the tone with five rules and two examples, the logo and three colours. The positioning was missing: everyone described the shop their own way. In Marketing Mix's Brand Simulation you write it yourself, starting from the data.

On social media the rules feel far away, but they exist, and they apply to small brands too. Here are the three to know from day one (the examples refer to Italy and the EU).

## 1. Advertising must be disclosed

If a creator (or an employee, or a friend) gets **money, products or other benefits** in exchange for content, that content is advertising. Viewers must understand it **immediately**:

- the platform's partnership label ("paid partnership" or similar);
- a clear wording **at the start** of the text: #ad, "Advertising", "In partnership with…";
- not buried among twenty hashtags at the end.

In Italy the reference is the Self-Regulatory Code of Commercial Communication (with its *Digital Chart*) and, for influencers with very large audiences, the guidelines and code of conduct approved by AGCOM in 2025, with a public register and fines of up to €250,000.

## 2. Prize contests

In Italy prize promotions are regulated by **Presidential Decree 430/2001**. An initiative promising prizes to promote products or a brand, with winners chosen by chance or skill, is a **prize contest**, even if entry is free and even if it runs on social media. Obligations include:

- written **rules**;
- **notifying the Ministry of Enterprises and Made in Italy** at least 15 days before the start;
- a **security deposit** equal to the value of the prizes;
- a notary or a Chamber of Commerce official for awarding the prizes.

Writing "this giveaway is not affiliated with the platform" isn't enough: that line is about the platform's rules, not Italian law.

## 3. Privacy

Comments, messages and sign-ups contain personal data. Under the **GDPR**:

- don't leave addresses, order numbers or phone numbers posted by mistake in public;
- if you collect contacts (for a contest, a newsletter), say what they're for and ask for consent when needed;
- respect anyone who asks not to be contacted any more.

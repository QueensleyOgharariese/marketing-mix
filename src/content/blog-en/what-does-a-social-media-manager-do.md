Behind a brand account that posts regularly, answers comments and always seems on point, there's almost always a **social media manager**. Not the person who "posts the photos": the person who decides why, for whom and with what result.

## What do they do every day?

1. Check overnight comments and messages, and answer the urgent ones.
2. Look at how the last few days' posts performed.
3. Prepare the week's content with designers, photographers or creators.
4. Schedule posts for when the audience is online.
5. Collect recurring questions for future content.

## The 3 parts of the job

- **Content**: choosing topics (the *pillars*), formats and calendar. See [how to build a social media content calendar](/en/blog/social-media-content-calendar/).
- **Community**: answering, moderating, passing requests to the right colleagues. See [community management](/en/blog/community-management/).
- **Numbers**: reading the analytics and deciding what to change. See [social media metrics](/en/blog/social-media-metrics/) and [engagement rate](/en/blog/engagement-rate/).

## Words to know

- **Reach**: how many different people saw a piece of content.
- **Impressions**: how many times it was seen in total.
- **Pillars**: the 3-5 topics the account always talks about.
- **Organic and paid**: normal posts and posts promoted with a budget.
- **UGC**: content made by customers, used only with their permission.

## Rules to know

- **Advertising must be disclosed**: if a creator gets money or products, the content is an ad and must be recognisable straight away (#ad, partnership label).
- **Other people's photos** aren't free to use: you need permission or a licence. Music too. See [user-generated content and rights](/en/blog/user-generated-content-and-rights/).
- **Giveaways** with prizes have specific rules in Italy (Presidential Decree 430/2001).
- **Personal data** in comments isn't left public.

## An example

Cinque Foglie, Marketing Mix's fictional tea shop, wants people to discover its new matcha kit. The social media manager checks the data: reels reach lots of people who don't follow the account, educational carousels get saved more. So: a reel to get it discovered, a carousel to explain what's inside, both scheduled for the evening when followers are online.

## Is it for you?

Probably, if at least two of these sound like you:

- You enjoy writing short texts and finding the right tone.
- You're curious about why one post works and another doesn't.
- You can stay calm when someone complains in public.
- You like organising: dates, ideas, priorities.

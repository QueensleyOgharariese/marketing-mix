Every time you open a social app you see different content from someone else. That's decided by the **algorithm**: a system that chooses, for each person, what to show and in what order.

## What it looks at

Algorithms aren't public in detail and change often, but the principles are known and similar across platforms:

- **Interest**: what kind of content that person usually watches.
- **Reactions**: how much people watch it (to the end?), whether they save it, share it, comment on it.
- **Relationship**: whether the person often interacts with that account.
- **Timing**: how recent the content is.

## The first push

New content is first shown to a group of people. If they react well, it's shown to more. That's why **timing** matters: post while your followers sleep and the first push is weak.

In the fictional Cinque Foglie shop, posts that went out at 3 am reached less than half as many people as similar posts at 7 pm.

## What you can do

1. Post when your audience is online (it's in your analytics).
2. Make content worth saving: guides, recipes, lists.
3. Grab attention in the first seconds of a video.
4. Reply to comments: conversations are a positive signal.
5. Measure and adjust: look at what works on your account, not generic tips. How to read the data is in [social media metrics](/en/blog/social-media-metrics/).

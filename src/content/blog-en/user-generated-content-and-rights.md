On social media everything feels like it belongs to everyone: one tap and you share it. For a business it doesn't work like that. Photos, videos and music have an author, and using them without permission can be costly.

## Customers' photos (UGC)

UGC stands for *user-generated content*. It's valuable because it's authentic, but it still **belongs to whoever made it**.

Before reposting:

1. Write to the person: introduce yourself, ask permission for a specific use, offer to credit them.
2. Let them say no.
3. Keep their reply.

In the fictional Cinque Foglie shop, a customer's photo had been taken from her profile and used without asking. The fix: archive the post, write to her apologising, repost only after she said yes.

## Photos found online

A photo found on an inspiration board or through an image search **isn't free to use**. Use only your own photos, licensed photos (free licences included, respecting their terms) or photos with written permission.

## Music

Business accounts must use **commercially licensed** music, such as the platforms' business music libraries. Popular songs often can't be used: the video may be muted or removed.

## Alt text

It's the image description read by screen readers, used by people who are blind or have low vision. Keep it simple and concrete: who, what, where. No "image of", no hashtags.

- **No:** "Our best kit ever! #matcha"
- **Yes:** "Hands whisking matcha with a bamboo whisk in a white bowl."

## Partnerships

If a creator gets money or products in exchange for content, it's advertising and must be clearly disclosed at the start (#ad, partnership label). Gifted products count too.

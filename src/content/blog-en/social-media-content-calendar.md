Posting "whenever" leads to busy weeks and empty weeks, the same topics over and over, and rushed posts. A **content calendar** fixes that: it's the plan of what goes out, where and when.

## What it contains

| Column | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Date and time | Wednesday 4 November, 7:30 pm |
| Channel | Main social account |
| Pillar | Learn about tea |
| Format | Carousel |
| Title or idea | How much water for matcha? |
| Goal | Get saved (useful content) |
| Status | Draft / Ready / Scheduled |

## The 5 steps

1. **Choose your pillars.** 3-5 fixed topics. For the fictional Cinque Foglie shop: Learn about tea, Recipes, Behind the scenes, Community, Product.
2. **Mark key dates.** Launches, holidays, sales. In November: the advent calendar launch on the 2nd, Black Friday on the 27th.
3. **Set the frequency.** For example 3 posts a week. Simple rule: never two posts on the same day.
4. **Pick posting times.** Check in your analytics when your followers are online: that's when a post gets its first push.
5. **Mix it up.** Not three product posts in a row: alternate pillars so the account stays useful and doesn't look like a flyer.

## Formats and goals

- **Short vertical videos**: usually reach the most new people.
- **Carousels**: great for step-by-step explanations, saved more often.
- **Single photos**: work when they tell a story, much less as plain ads.

## A mistake to avoid

Scheduling tools have a **default time**: if you schedule lots of posts at once without choosing one, they all use it. In the fictional Cinque Foglie shop it was set to 3 am: for weeks posts went out at night and reach halved. Always check the date and time of every queued post.

## Then

Once a month, look at how posts did by pillar and format, and shift the calendar towards what works. What to look at is explained in [social media metrics](/en/blog/social-media-metrics/).

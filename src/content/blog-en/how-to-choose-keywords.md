Before writing a page you need to know **what people type** when they look for that thing. That's keyword research: the first task in every SEO project.

## What is a keyword?

A keyword is the word or phrase someone types into Google. It can be short, like "green tea", or long, like "how to make green tea without it turning bitter".

You don't choose it on instinct: you look at a few pieces of data, always the same ones.

## The 4 things to look at

| Data | What it tells you | Where to find it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Volume** | How many times a month it's searched | Keyword Planner, SEO tools |
| **Difficulty** | How hard it is to reach the first page | SEO tools (estimate) |
| **Intent** | What the person searching wants to do | By looking at Google's results |
| **Trend** | Which months it's searched most | Google Trends |

Volumes are always **estimates**: use them to compare keywords with each other, not as exact numbers.

## Short and long keywords

- **Short** ("tea"): huge numbers of searches, mixed intents, very strong competition.
- **Long** ("organic loose leaf green tea"): fewer searches, but people typing them already know what they want.

For a young website, long keywords are the starting point: you reach the top sooner and the visits are more useful. They're also called *long tail* keywords.

## A step-by-step example

Cinque Foglie is Marketing Mix's fictional tea shop. It needs a keyword for its green tea category page.

1. **Start with a list.** Write how a customer would search: "green tea", "organic green tea", "buy green tea", "green tea benefits", "sencha".
2. **Check the volumes.** "Green tea" has a huge number of searches, "organic green tea" far fewer.
3. **Check the intent.** Search "green tea benefits" on Google: articles come up, not shops. It's not right for a page that sells. Intent has its own article: [search intent explained simply](/en/blog/search-intent/).
4. **Look at the trend.** Searches for hot teas rise from October, iced teas in summer: improve the page before the peak.
5. **Choose.** For the category: "organic green tea". For a blog article: "green tea benefits", written without health claims.

## One keyword, one page

If two pages on the same site target the same keyword, Google doesn't know which one to show and often shows **both lower down**. This is called **keyword cannibalisation**.

The fix is a simple map: each page with its main keyword, written in a spreadsheet. Before creating a new page, check that the keyword isn't already "taken".

## Common mistakes

- Choosing only by volume.
- Using the same keyword for several pages.
- Translating keywords word for word from another language: people search differently in every language.
- Stuffing the text with repeated keywords: Google notices, and readers leave.

Every Google result has two texts that you write: the **title tag** and the **meta description**. They're the first thing a person reads, even before entering your website.

## What is the title tag?

It's the blue, clickable title of the result. It tells Google and people what the page is about, and it's one of the most important signals for SEO.

Google doesn't count letters: it measures how much space they take up. An "m" is wider than an "i", so two titles with the same number of characters can have different widths. On desktop, beyond about **580 pixels** the title gets cut off with "...".

## How do you write a good title tag?

- Put the **keyword at the start**: it's the part people read first.
- Put the **brand at the end**, separated by a bar: "| Cinque Foglie".
- **Really** describe the page: no exaggerated promises or repeated words.
- Each page has **its own** title, different from the other pages.

## What is the meta description?

It's the grey text under the title. It doesn't move the page up in the rankings, but it helps people decide whether to click. A good length is **between 70 and 160 characters**: enough to say something useful, not so much that it gets cut off.

## A before and after example

| | Title tag | Meta description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Before** | Sencha | Green tea. Buy now. |
| **After** | Organic Loose Leaf Sencha Green Tea \| Cinque Foglie | Organic Japanese Sencha with whole leaves and a fresh, grassy taste. Great iced too. 24-hour delivery. |

## Mind the rules

For food, in the European Union health claims ("detoxes", "helps you lose weight") are only allowed if they are authorised (Regulation (EC) No 1924/2006). This applies to title tags and meta descriptions too: talk about taste, quality and service.

21|A brand isn't the logo
22|A brand is the idea people have of a company: what it promises, how it makes them feel, why they'd choose it. The logo is just one of the signs that make it recognisable. A brand is built with everything people come across: products, packaging, website, customer service, way of writing.

26|Identity and image
27|Brand identity is how the company wants to present itself: name, logo, colours, typefaces, tone of voice, values. Brand image is how people actually see it. The brand manager's job is to bring the two closer: if the company wants to be friendly but customers find it cold, something isn't working.

36|Positioning
37|Positioning is the place the brand wants to hold in people's minds compared with competitors. It's summed up in one sentence: who it's for, what need it meets and why it's different. It helps decide what to do and what not to do: a brand can't be everything to everyone.

45|Promise and values
46|The brand promise is what customers can expect every time. Values are the principles that guide choices. They only count if they show in actions: a brand that calls itself sustainable but ships in parcels full of plastic loses credibility. 76|Visual identity and tone of voice
77|Visual identity is the set of graphic signs: logo, colours, typefaces, style of photos and illustrations. It's always used the same way, so the brand is recognisable even without reading the name. The logo has rules: clear space around it, minimum sizes, versions for light and dark backgrounds, what not to do (stretch it, change its colours).

85|Tone of voice
86|Tone of voice is the way the brand speaks: the words it chooses, how formal it is, whether it's informal or formal with the reader, whether it jokes. It's described with three or four adjectives and examples of what to say and what not to say. The tone stays the same, but adapts to the situation: a post can be light, a reply to a complaint can't.

94|The brand manual
95|The brand manual (brand guidelines or brand book) collects all the rules: positioning, values, logo, colours with their codes, typefaces, image style, tone of voice with examples. It's for anyone working on the brand, including people outside the company: agencies, photographers, new colleagues. Without a manual, everyone interprets it their own way.

99|Consistency across all channels
100|A person meets the brand at many points: a post, the packaging, the website, an email, customer service. If each point seems to belong to a different company, the brand doesn't stick in memory. Consistency doesn't mean making everything identical, but making everything sound like the same person.

131|Measuring and protecting the brand
132|Awareness
133|Brand awareness measures how many people know the brand. It's measured with surveys: unaided awareness («which online tea shops do you know?») and aided awareness («do you know Cinque Foglie?»). Unaided is harder to achieve and worth more. A free clue is how many people search for the brand name on Google.

141|How to measure a brand
142|What people think
143|Besides knowing whether people know us, we need to know what they think. Sentiment is the tone of what's said about the brand online: positive, neutral or negative. NPS (Net Promoter Score) asks «how likely are you to recommend this brand to a friend, from 0 to 10?»: people answering 9-10 are promoters, people answering 0 to 6 are detractors. NPS = % promoters − % detractors.
144|Out of 100 customers, 50 give 9-10, 30 give 7-8 and 20 give 0 to 6: NPS = 50 − 20 = 30.
145|Out of 200 answers, 100 are promoters and 40 detractors. What's the NPS?
146|50% promoters − 20% detractors = 30.
147|Protecting the trademark
148|The name and logo can be registered as a trademark: that way nobody else can use them for similar products. In Italy you register with the UIBM, for the whole European Union with the EUIPO. Before choosing a new name, check that no similar trademark already exists in the same sector. Protecting also means using other people's trademarks properly: you don't use other companies' logos or names as if they were your own.
149|Cinque Foglie wants to call a range «Foglie d'Oro»: before printing the packaging, it checks the trademark databases to make sure the name is free for tea.
150|A trademark registered with the EUIPO is protected in every European Union country.
151|The EU trade mark is valid in all member states.

153|When something goes wrong
154|A reputation crisis can come from a real mistake (a faulty product) or from an online controversy. The basic rules: reply quickly, tell the truth, apologise if the mistake is ours, explain what's being done to fix it, use the same tone on every channel. Deleting critical comments usually makes things worse.

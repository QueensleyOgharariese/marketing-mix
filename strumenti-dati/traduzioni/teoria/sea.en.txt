20|What SEA is and how the auction works
21|Search engine ads
22|SEA (Search Engine Advertising) is advertising on search engines. When someone searches, ads labelled «Sponsored» appear above and below the organic results. With SEA you only pay when someone clicks: that's why it's called pay-per-click (PPC). Unlike SEO, ads appear straight away, but they stop appearing as soon as you stop paying.

29|The auction: who appears, and in what order
30|Every time someone searches, Google runs an auction between the ads that want to appear. It's not just the highest bid that wins: ad quality also counts, meaning how relevant the ad is to the search and how useful the page it leads to is. A very relevant ad can rank higher while paying less than a competitor.

80|Match types
81|For each keyword you choose how closely it must match the search. Exact match: the ad appears for searches with the same meaning. Phrase match: it appears for searches that include the keyword's meaning. Broad match: it also appears for related searches, and it's the widest. The broader it is, the more people it reaches, but the more it risks the wrong searches.

99|Writing an ad
100|Search ads are «responsive»: you write up to 15 headlines (max 30 characters each) and up to 4 descriptions (max 90 characters), and Google combines the ones that work best. A good ad echoes the search, states a concrete benefit and invites action. It can't promise false things, nor, for a food, unauthorised health effects.

132|Budget, numbers and rules
133|A campaign's numbers
134|Impressions: how many times the ad appears. Clicks and CTR: how many people click out of 100 who see it. CPC: average cost of a click. Conversions: the actions that matter, usually purchases. Conversion rate: how many clicks turn into a purchase. Together they show where you're losing people: if few click, it's the ad; if they click but don't buy, it's the page or the product.
135|100 clicks, 3 purchases: conversion rate 3%. If clicks rise to 200 but purchases stay at 3, something's wrong with the page or the keywords.

144|CPA and ROAS: is the campaign paying off?
145|CPA (cost per acquisition) is how much a sale costs on average: spend divided by conversions. ROAS (return on ad spend) is how much you take in for every euro spent: revenue divided by spend. A ROAS of 4 means €4 in sales for every euro of ads. Whether it's worth it depends on the margin: what the shop keeps after product and shipping costs.
146|The iced teas campaign spends €500 and brings €2,000 in sales: ROAS 4. With 25 orders, the CPA is 500 / 25 = €20.
147|You spend €300 and take in €1,500. What's the ROAS?
148|1,500 / 300 = 5: €5 in sales for every euro spent.
149|You spend €400 and get 20 orders. What's the CPA?
150|400 / 20 = €20 per order.
151|Budget and bidding
152|The budget is set per day, per campaign: it's an average, and on some days Google may spend a bit more and on others less. Bidding can be manual (you decide how much to pay per click) or automated: Google uses data to reach a goal, for example a target CPA or ROAS. Automated bidding only works well if conversions are measured correctly.

156|Measuring sales and EU rules
157|To know which ads sell, you measure conversions with a code on the website. In the European Union, cookies that aren't needed for the site to work require the person's consent (cookie banner, GDPR and the ePrivacy directive); Google asks European sites to pass on the choice made in the banner. UTM links are also used to understand where visits come from. And ads follow the same rules as any advertising: no false or misleading promises.

22|A brand uses social media for different goals: getting known (awareness), building a relationship with people (community), bringing visits to the website and sales, giving support. Each goal is measured differently, so it has to be chosen before publishing. The social media manager works mainly on organic content, meaning content that isn't sponsored.

32|Each platform has its own audience
33|Platforms differ in audience and format. Instagram lives on photos, carousels and short videos (reels). 40|Organic and paid
41|Organic content is published for free and mainly reaches people who follow the account or who get it from the algorithm. Sponsored content (social ads) is paid for to reach people chosen by age, interests, location. It's often handled by another role or an agency, but the social media manager chooses which content deserves a boost.

45|How the algorithm works
46|Every platform decides what to show each person with an algorithm that tries to predict what will interest them. It looks at signals such as how long people spend on content, whether they watch to the end, and whether they save, share or comment. There's no secret trick: content people find useful or beautiful gets shown more.

87|Formats
88|The same theme can be told in different formats. Short vertical videos (reels, TikTok) to get discovered. Carousels, meaning several images to swipe through, to explain step by step: they get saved often. Stories, which last 24 hours, for everyday updates and quick questions. Single posts for a strong photo or an announcement. You choose the format based on what you want to achieve.

98|The content calendar
99|The content calendar says what gets published, where and when, usually a few weeks ahead. It brings together key dates (launches, holidays, seasons), pillars and formats. Posting regularly matters more than posting a lot. Always leave some room for things that happen unexpectedly.

107|Other people's content, rights and accessibility
108|Content created by customers (UGC, user-generated content) is valuable, but before reposting it you ask for permission and credit the author. Music and images have rights too: only use those with a suitable licence, such as the platforms' music libraries for commercial use. Good content is also accessible: subtitles on videos and image descriptions (alt text) for people who can't see.

140|Metrics
141|Reach: how many different people saw a piece of content. Impressions: how many times it was seen in total, including repeat views by the same person. Interactions: likes, comments, saves, shares. Link clicks: how many people went to the website. Follower growth: how many new followers in the period. Saves and shares usually say more than likes, because they take more effort.

151|Social media metrics: which to watch and when
152|Engagement rate
153|Engagement rate shows how much people react to content: interactions divided by reach, times 100. It can also be calculated on followers, which is useful for comparing different accounts, but when you know the reach it's better to use it. There's no right value for everyone: compare with your own previous posts.

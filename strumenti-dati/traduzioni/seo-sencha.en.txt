89|Keywords and search intent
90|A keyword is the search a person types into Google. Behind every search there's an intent: to learn, to choose between products or to buy. A product page should target people who want to choose or buy; people who want to learn are looking for a guide.
91|"how to clean a cast iron teapot" is for learning (it needs a guide); "1 litre cast iron teapot" is for choosing (it needs the product page).
92|Title and meta description
93|The title is the blue title in Google's results; the meta description is the grey text underneath. The title must contain the keyword and stay within about 580 pixels, otherwise it gets cut. The brand name usually goes at the end. The description makes people want to click.
94|"0.8 l cast iron teapot, enamelled inside | Casa del Tè" is clear; "Casa del Tè | Products" doesn't say what's on the page.
95|Structured data
96|These are lines of code (JSON-LD) that people don't see, but Google does: they state precisely the product's name, brand, price and availability. They must match what's written on the page. Stars can only be shown with real reviews.
97|If the page says €39 and the structured data says €29, Google notices and may not show the price.
98|Short answers
99|Google often shows short answers to questions, in the "People also ask" box or in answers generated by artificial intelligence. An answer of 30-70 words, precise and with the right numbers, is more likely to be chosen.

115|A social media manager decides what to publish, in which format and when, replies to the community and looks at the numbers to understand what works. What counts isn't how many people follow you, but how many really interact with what you publish.
116|Formats
117|Each format has a job. The Reel (short video) shows an action and also reaches people who don't follow you. The carousel (several images to swipe) suits lists and explanations to save. Stories last 24 hours and are for quick things and getting people to take part with polls and questions.
118|A teapot shop makes a Reel showing how to clean cast iron, a carousel "3 teapots for 3 types of tea" and a Story with the poll "Cast iron or glass?".
119|Engagement rate
120|It measures how much people interact. You add up the interactions (likes, comments, saves) and divide by followers, then multiply by 100. Saves count a lot: they mean "I need this, I'll look at it again".
121|3,000 followers; a post gets 90 likes, 15 comments and 15 saves = 120 interactions. 120 ÷ 3,000 = 0.04, i.e. 4%.
122|The caption
123|The first line is the hook: it must spark curiosity, because the rest is hidden. Then useful information and an invitation to do something (call to action), like saving or commenting. A few relevant hashtags work better than thirty generic ones.
124|"Is your cast iron teapot rusting? Here's how to avoid it. Save the post for next time. #teapot #tea"
125|Rules and community
126|Replying to comments is part of the job. For food in Europe you can't promise unauthorised health effects, not even in a comment. When faced with health questions you reply kindly and suggest a doctor.
127|To "Does this herbal tea get rid of headaches?" you reply talking about the taste and suggesting asking a doctor, not with a yes.

128|People in PR (public relations) look after the company's reputation: they make true news known to journalists and the public and, when something goes wrong, respond quickly and honestly. You don't buy articles: you give journalists news that interests their readers.
129|What news is
130|For a journalist, news is a fact that's new, true and interesting to their readers: it touches the local area, people, numbers. Opinions, self-praise and small internal changes aren't news.
131|A bakery that opens a second shop and hires 4 people is news for the city paper; "our bread is the best" isn't.
132|The press release
133|It's the text you send to journalists. The first lines (the lead) say straight away who, what, where and when, with verifiable data. The rest adds details, a quote from someone in charge and the contacts.
134|"From 3 March, Panificio Grano Vivo opens its second shop at Via Roma 12 in Padua and hires 4 people."
135|Sending it well
136|You send it to a few journalists who follow that topic, on a working day, with real photos and a contact. If they don't reply you make one polite follow-up at most. Bought address lists and gifts in exchange for articles aren't used.
137|The bakery writes to Padua's local news and business journalists, not to every newspaper in Italy.
138|Handling a crisis
139|When something goes wrong you reply early, publicly, acknowledging the problem; you explain what happened and what you're doing to fix it. You don't delete criticism and you never publish customers' personal data.
140|"Today our oven broke down and some deliveries will arrive tomorrow: we apologise, everyone who ordered will get a voucher."

121|using data correctly
122|Market research is for deciding with data instead of hunches. People who do it prepare questions that don't influence the answers, choose the right people to ask, read the results carefully and respect participants' privacy.
123|Well-designed questions
124|A good question is neutral (it doesn't suggest the answer), asks one thing and collects only the data really needed: under the GDPR personal data must be kept to a minimum.
125|An ice cream shop asks "How often do you eat ice cream in winter?", not "Don't you think ice cream in winter is fantastic too?".
126|The sample
127|It's the group of people you ask. It must resemble the customers you want to reach and be large enough. Asking only friends or only fans creates a bias: the results come out more positive than reality.
128|For a new ice cream, 300 people who eat ice cream at least once a month, half customers and half not.
129|Reading the results
130|Answers are turned into percentages (answers ÷ total × 100). Careful: "I'd buy it" is an intention, not a purchase. Real sales are usually lower than what people say.
131|90 people out of 300 say they'd try the new flavour: 90 ÷ 300 = 30%. It doesn't mean 30% will really buy it.
132|After the research
133|Before investing a lot you run a small test and listen to the objections. The data collected is used only for the stated purpose, and results aren't touched up to look better.

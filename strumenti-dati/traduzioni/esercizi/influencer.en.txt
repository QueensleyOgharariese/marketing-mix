114|People in influencer marketing choose the creators to work with, write the instructions (the brief), check that content follows the rules and measure whether the partnership earned more than it cost.
115|Choosing the right creator
116|Three things count before followers: that the audience resembles our customers, lives where we sell and really interacts (high engagement). A huge profile with a distant audience brings in less than a small, loyal one.
117|To sell ceramic mugs in Milan, a ceramicist with 15k Italian followers and 5% engagement is worth more than a comedian with a million followers abroad.
118|The brief
119|It's the written instructions for the creator: goal, message, what to show, dates and rules to follow. The clearer it is, the fewer the surprises. But the creator must stay free to speak in their own voice.
120|"Goal: introduce the handmade mugs. Show how you use them at breakfast. Disclose the partnership. Publish by the 15th."
121|Disclosing advertising
122|In Italy and the European Union viewers must understand straight away that content is advertising. It's written clearly and at the start, for example "#ad" or "Advertising". Hiding the partnership or calling it a "gift" is covert advertising, which is banned.
123|Right: "Advertising · In partnership with @tazzefattemano" in the first line. Wrong: "#collab" among twenty hashtags.
124|Measuring the result
125|With a personal discount code you count the orders the creator brought. From there you calculate revenue (orders × average value), cost per order (fee ÷ orders) and ROAS (revenue ÷ fee).
126|Fee €300, 40 orders of €25: revenue €1,000, cost per order €7.50, ROAS = 1,000 ÷ 300 ≈ 3.3.

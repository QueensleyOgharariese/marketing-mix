94|A clear written agreement (tasks, money, duration, trademarks, data) avoids misunderstandings. For a real agreement always ask a lawyer: here you only learn what mustn't be missing.
95|Who does what, costs and payments, duration and ending, use of logos. No customer data used freely, no verbal-only agreements, no exclusivity forever.

113|what to put in an agreement
114|People in partnerships look for other companies to do something together that neither could do alone: a joint product, a promotion, an event. A good partnership benefits both, is measurable and is written in a clear agreement.
115|The right partner
116|The ideal partner sells something that goes well with our product (complementary), has customers similar to ours and compatible values. A direct competitor or a company with distant values isn't a good fit, even if it's big.
117|An independent bookshop can team up with a neighbourhood café: people who read often drink coffee. Not with another bookshop: they sell the same thing.
118|The proposal
119|A good proposal creates something new for both companies' customers, splits the work and can be measured (for example with one code each). Customer lists are never swapped: under the GDPR their consent would be needed.
120|Bookshop and café: "a book + a coffee" on sale in both places, with a different code to count who sells more.
121|The maths
122|You add up all the costs of one item, calculate the margin (price − costs) and split it as agreed. Then you multiply by the items you expect to sell. Hidden costs, like time, are left out.
123|Box at €25; book €6, coffee €4, packaging €5: cost €15, margin €10, €5 each. With 100 boxes: €500 each.
124|The written agreement
125|Before starting you write down who does what, how costs and earnings are split, how long it lasts, how logos are used and who handles customer data. Exclusivity forever and verbal-only agreements cause problems. For contracts it's best to get help from a lawyer.
126|The bookshop and the café write that the partnership lasts until January and that each uses only its own customers' data.

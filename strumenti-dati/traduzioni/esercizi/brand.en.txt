101|How do I recognise an off-tone sentence?
102|Compare it with the personality on the card: kind, clear, expert, never pushy. If it rushes people, exaggerates or attacks someone, it's off tone.
103|What is contrast?
104|The difference in brightness between text and background. The higher it is, the easier it is to read. Normal text needs at least 4.5:1.
105|Tone of voice: the way a brand speaks. Values: the things it believes in, proven by facts. Contrast: how much the text stands out from the background.

117|A brand is the idea people form of a company: not just the logo, but how it speaks, what it believes in and how it behaves. A brand manager writes the rules that keep everything consistent, so that a post, an email or a reply to a customer seem written by the same person.
118|Tone of voice
119|It's the way the brand speaks: formal or friendly, calm or shouted, technical or simple. It comes from the brand's personality and is used everywhere, from the website to comments.
120|A kind, passionate neighbourhood bookshop writes "We recommend a mystery for tonight's rain", not "MEGA BOOK DEAL!!!".
121|Values
122|They're the things the brand believes in. They only count if the facts prove them: saying "sustainable" without being so is misleading. Generic words like "excellence" or "passion" are used by everyone and set nobody apart.
123|The bookshop that runs free readings for children can say it believes in culture for everyone: there's a fact that proves it.
124|Visual rules
125|Colours, typefaces and logo have precise rules. An important one is contrast: text must stand out clearly from the background, otherwise many people can't read it (accessibility guidelines ask for at least 4.5 to 1 for normal text).
126|Light grey text on a white background is elegant but hard to read; almost-black text on white is always readable.
127|The brand in difficult moments
128|Tone shows most when something goes wrong. A good reply acknowledges the problem, genuinely apologises and offers a solution, without shifting the blame and without joking about an inconvenience.
129|A book arrives damaged: "We're sorry, we'll send you a new copy today" works; "It must be the courier's fault" doesn't.

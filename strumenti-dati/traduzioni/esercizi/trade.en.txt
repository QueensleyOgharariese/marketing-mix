123|working with the buyer
124|Trade marketing is about selling through other people's stores, like supermarkets. People in this role negotiate with the chain's buyer, choose where and how to sit on the shelf, prepare promotions and check store by store whether the product sells.
125|The shelf
126|In the store people decide in a few seconds. The best spot is at eye level, in the aisle where people look for that type of product. Extra display stands (near the checkouts, at the end of an aisle) help at the right moments.
127|An artisan jam sells more at eye level, next to the other organic jams, than on the bottom shelf.
128|Sell-in and sell-out
129|Sell-in is how much we sell to the supermarket (the packs delivered). Sell-out is how much the supermarket sells to customers. To stay on the shelf sell-out counts: if products don't move, the buyer removes them.
130|We deliver 10 jars of jam to 20 stores: sell-in 200. If customers buy 150 in a month, the average sell-out is 150 ÷ 20 = 7.5 per store.
131|Store margin
132|The supermarket pays us a sell price and resells at a higher price. The difference is its margin: the store needs to earn too, otherwise it won't keep us.
133|Jam sold to the store at €3.00, sold on at €4.50: the supermarket earns €1.50 per jar.
134|Compliant promotions
135|A good promotion is simple, true and time-limited. You can't say "the lowest price" without checking it, and a free gift can't be exchanged for personal data without freely given consent.
136|"2 jars for €8 instead of €9, from 1 to 15 March" is clear and verifiable.

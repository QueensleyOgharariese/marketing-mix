62|Without UTMs, newsletter visits get mixed up with the others. With UTMs always written the same way (lowercase, hyphens, clear names) reports become readable.

106|Martech is the set of marketing tools: analytics, CRM, email, advertising. They need to be set up properly and within the rules: in Italy the data protection authority's cookie guidelines (2021) require rejecting to be as easy as accepting, and scrolling not to count as consent.
107|A banner with Accept and Reject at the same level, tools started only after consent, shared UTM rules. No hidden Reject, no consent by scrolling, no customer data in open sheets.
108|There can be more than one right answer. Keep the choices that respect consent (equal buttons, tools started only after a yes) and the ones that bring order (shared UTM rules). Discard tricks to make people accept and any unsafe use of data.
109|A banner with a big orange "Accept" and a small grey "Reject" pushes people to accept: it's not freely given consent.
110|How do I read the funnel?
111|Divide each step by the step before (carts ÷ visits, checkouts ÷ carts, orders ÷ checkouts) and compare it with the typical value on the card.
112|How do you write a UTM?
113|Three labels: source (who sends it: newsletter), medium (the type of channel: email) and campaign (the campaign name). Always lowercase, with hyphens instead of spaces.
114|Channel: where a visit comes from. UTM: labels added to a link to recognise where it comes from. Funnel: the steps from visit to order. Conversion rate: how many take the action out of how many could. Martech: marketing's technology tools.
